The Diastase Secret: Why Temperature Dictates Honey's True Healing Power

Dr. Althea Rossi, Head Extraction Biologist
• September 4, 2026 • 6 min read

When consumers enter an ordinary supermarket, 95% of the golden syrup sitting in plastic squeeze bears is biologically dead. To prevent honey from naturally crystallizing on the shelf, commercial packers subject the nectar to flash-pasteurization at temperatures reaching 165°F (74°C) before pressing it through pressurized diatomaceous earth filters.

While this produces a clear, eternally liquid syrup, it completely destroys honey's primary medicinal constituents: the live enzymes diastase, invertase, and glucose oxidase.

"Honey is not merely an alternative sweetener; it is a bio-active matrix produced by honeybee hypopharyngeal glands containing over 180 identified bioactive compounds that deteriorate irreversibly when subjected to heat."

What is Diastase Activity?

In European apicultural standards, the freshness and raw integrity of honey is quantified by the Diastase Number (DN). Diastase (alpha- and beta-amylase) is an enzyme added to nectar by honeybees during processing. It breaks down complex starches into maltose and glucose.

Because diastase is exceptionally sensitive to heat degradation, international food safety bodies recognize any honey with a Diastase Number below 8 as overheated or adulterated. At Amber & Oak, our cold-gravity extraction ensures our harvests consistently test at 24 to 28 DN.

The Glucose Oxidase Mechanism

Perhaps the most miraculous component of raw honey is glucose oxidase. In the presence of oxygen and body moisture, this active enzyme continuously releases minute, physiological concentrations of organic hydrogen peroxide. This is why medical-grade raw honey has been documented in peer-reviewed clinical literature for accelerating burn tissue regeneration and eradicating antibiotic-resistant bacteria without irritating healthy dermis.

Key Takeaways for Your Kitchen
  • Never dissolve raw honey in boiling hot water or tea; allow your beverage to cool to drinking warmth (<105°F) before stirring in honey.
  • Embrace crystallization! True raw honey with high floral pollen naturally granulates over time into a decadent creamy texture.
  • Store your honey jar at ambient room temperature in a dark pantry away from direct oven heat.
